Social Housing Deadlines Reshaping UK Energy Demand

*Four deadlines that can't move, and one scheme that currently excludes you.*

*If you're coming at this from the grid or demand-flexibility side rather than housing compliance: these dates aren't just a regulatory calendar. They're a set of dated forcing functions sitting directly on top of a large, currently gas-and-storage-heated slice of UK residential demand — the kind of thing that normally shows up in capacity auctions or connection queues, not tenant-protection legislation. Read it as a timeline for when a meaningful chunk of demand-side behaviour has to change, whether or not the market is ready to make that change affordable yet.*

This series has spent months on the carbon and cost case for getting storage-heated homes off gas. Underneath all of that sits a simpler, more urgent question a lot of asset managers and compliance leads across social housing are quietly working through right now: what actually has to happen, and by when. Here's the real, dated picture, in order.

**30 November 2026: excess cold — and excess heat — both become legal hazards, in the same law, on the same day.**

This is the one this whole series has been circling, and it's worth being precise about what actually happens on this date. Awaab's Law Phase 1 (October 2025) covered only damp, mould, and emergency hazards. Phase 2, arriving 30 November 2026, adds seven more hazard categories in one go — including excess cold and excess heat together, not as separate milestones. From that date, social landlords have to treat excess cold in a home as a legal hazard — not a tenant lifestyle choice, not something to note and revisit next year. If any of a social landlord's stock is storage-heated and genuinely under-heated because the running cost is too high for the tenant to manage, that's now a compliance exposure, not just a comfort issue.

The excess heat half of the same date is worth flagging separately, because it's the less-discussed one: the social-housing overheating guidance I've been able to find is almost entirely about insulation, shading, and ventilation, with nothing on mechanical cooling. If any social landlord's stock struggles with summer heat as much as winter cold, that's the easier half of this deadline to miss — not because it's a different date, but because it's the newer half of the same law.

**31 March 2028: the grant money has to be spent — not the same date as the funding window closing.**

The Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund's Wave 3 allocation — £1.29bn across 144 projects — has a real spend deadline, and it's earlier than the headline delivery window suggests: all grant funding must be spent by 31 March 2028, with co-funding usable only in the final six months of a delivery window that runs to September 2028. For any social landlord holding an allocation, the clock on actually spending the grant is tighter than "2025 to 2028" makes it sound. One thing worth checking directly against the application rather than assuming: neither heat pumps nor storage-heater upgrades are named as their own distinct eligible category in what's publicly published about the fund — it's worth confirming exactly what specific measures qualify as before building a delivery plan around an assumption.

**2030, then 2039: the big one has two dates inside it, and the first has a genuine open question.**

MEES compliance for social housing actually runs in two stages: a first metric due by 1 April 2030, and a second by 1 April 2039. For each stage, landlords choose one of three routes — Fabric Performance, Smart Readiness, or Heating System — hit it, and that stage is compliant, without needing all three at once. Each route carries a £10,000 spend cap per property, which matters a great deal for exactly the flats-dominant, solid-wall-heavy stock a lot of storage-heated social housing sits in, since fabric work alone can exceed that cap on some properties.

Here's the genuinely open part, and it sits inside the 2030 date specifically: the technical definition of what counts as "Smart Readiness" hasn't been finalised. It's waiting on a separate Home Energy Model / EPC consultation, which closed for comment on 18 March 2026, with a government response covering the exact thresholds and qualifying measures still expected only as "later in 2026" — no firm date set as of this writing. What we do know from the consultation's own illustrative modelling is that it assumed a smart metric would involve solar PV, batteries, or smart-tariff-enabling smart meters — demand-response software and storage-heater controls specifically were not named. For any landlord leaning a 2030 compliance strategy on the Smart Readiness route, that's a real, dated thing worth watching for rather than assuming settled, because the rules that decide whether planned measures actually count haven't been published yet.

**The scheme that sounds like it should help, but currently doesn't: the Boiler Upgrade Scheme.**

BUS was extended to 2030 in its April 2026 refresh, and picked up a new £2,500 grant for air-to-air heat pumps alongside its existing £7,500 air-to-water grant. None of that reaches social housing: BUS guidance is explicit that "the scheme is not open to social housing providers," and the exclusion isn't technology-specific — air-to-air is excluded exactly like every other type. This isn't a deadline to hit; it's a scheme worth not wasting time applying to right now, and worth watching, since the exclusion is a policy choice rather than a technical limitation, and policy choices are the one category of barrier in this whole series that's actually reversible.

**Putting it in order:** 30 November this year is immovable and already close, and it lands on cold and heat together, not in two separate waves. 2028 is a real spending deadline hiding a tighter date (31 March) behind a softer-sounding funding window. 2030 is the deadline everyone's already heard about, with a compliance route (Smart Readiness) whose actual rules aren't written yet — and a second, less-discussed deadline (2039) waiting behind it. And BUS is the scheme that looks like an answer but isn't one for this stock, at least not today.

None of these dates move because the underlying technology or funding picture is uncertain. Planning around them now, even with the Smart Readiness gap still open, beats finding out in 2029 which route was actually eligible.
