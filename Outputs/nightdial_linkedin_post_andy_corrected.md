Winter's coming. The data shows why storage‑heaters stay cold. And why point heating still wins, despite the mould.

Smart‑meter data shows the cost gap that drives people to switch storage heaters off for point heating instead.

We found a storage heater costs £313 more than gas even under perfect optimisation. Turning it down isn't a mistake — it's a rational response to a structural price gap: point heating comes on, whole‑home heating stays off, and mould risk rises with every cold spell.

I pulled apart half‑hourly smart‑meter data from 4,443 London households, isolated genuine storage‑heater homes by their overnight charging signature, and measured how much of that charging the night's temperature actually justified.

We found 12.2% of all overnight charging was useless. At today's tariffs, that's £117 a year burned into a hot brick that didn't need to be hot. If your heater wastes money, you turn it down and reach for point heating instead — leaving the cold patches that become mould risk.

Roughly 1.7 million UK homes still rely on storage heating. Around 136,500 are in housing associations. National Energy Action, a fuel‑poverty charity, has warned for years that outdated storage heaters trap tenants in cold, damp homes. The smart‑meter data shows the mechanism behind that claim — and it's not just old dial units. Even newer "smart" Qantum heaters are being switched off in favour of point heating and electric blankets, for the same reason.

I also tested the obvious fix: "put them on a smart dynamic tariff." It doesn't work. An unmanaged heater can't respond to price signals — it charges through the expensive peaks and misses the cheap troughs. Switching to Octopus Agile made costs slightly worse, not better. The saving was never in the tariff — it's in the timing, and these heaters don't have any.

Same two‑bed flat, real prices: gas comes in at £378 a year. An unmanaged storage heater costs £927. Even with perfect optimisation — zero waste, perfectly timed charging — the least you can pay is £691. That's the £313 gap, baked into the physics and the tariffs, not the behaviour.

People aren't being careless. They're responding to a price signal they can feel. Peer‑reviewed evidence shows prepayment‑meter households self‑disconnect more during cold spells, not less — a pattern housing associations must now act on under Awaab's Law.

People have been saying "make storage heaters smarter" for years. What's been missing is measurement: real data, real prices, and results that include the awkward parts — the waste, the negative tariff test, the gap no optimisation can close.

There's real scope for better timing, better controls, and better outcomes. I won't get far on my own.
