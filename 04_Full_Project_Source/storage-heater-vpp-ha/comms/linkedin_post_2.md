Follow-up to my last post on storage heaters: I spent a few hours trying to prove my own project pointless. Here's what happened.

The obvious challenge to everything I found: UK social housing has to hit EPC C by 2030 (that's now confirmed government policy, not a proposal — announced this January). Add solar, a battery, maybe a heat pump, and the smart-control retrofit I've been building might just be... moot. A rounding error next to bigger fixes already coming.

So I checked. Properly, with numbers.

**Insulation does bite — hard.** If a flat actually reaches EPC C, the pound saving from smart control roughly halves. Not disappears — halves. £236 a year down to somewhere around £118–153. Worth knowing before anyone builds a business case on today's figures for a 2030 stock.

**Solar and battery don't compete with this at all — they solve a different problem.** A typical 4kW solar system's entire December output is less than one household's single night of storage-heater charging. The real, substantial saving from solar (£600-1,300/year, genuinely worth having) comes almost entirely from spring and summer, offsetting the general electricity bill — not winter heating. Two different problems, two different seasons, not a competition.

**Then I found something I wasn't looking for.** Storage heater running costs aren't smooth across the year — 53% of a whole year's charging happens in just four winter months. So a £927 "annual" unmanaged storage heater bill is actually more like £123 a month for four straight months, not the £77 an annual average implies. That gap matters enormously if your income doesn't seasonally adjust — and there's already peer-reviewed data showing prepayment customers self-disconnect *more* during cold snaps, not less, despite needing heat most. Over a third now live in homes they describe as cold and damp as a direct result.

That's not an abstract cost problem anymore. That's the mechanism behind exactly the kind of damp and mould risk Awaab's Law now makes housing associations legally responsible for acting on.

I even checked heat pumps properly rather than wave them off. Running costs looked genuinely good — competitive with gas. Then I found the catch: the main government grant explicitly excludes social housing, and most of these flats have no radiators to connect one to in the first place. Real option, real complications, not yet a settled case either way.

None of this makes the hardware project worthless. But it does mean the honest pitch to a housing association isn't "this saves you £X forever" — it's "this closes a real, currently-uncovered gap, and the size of that gap is shrinking on a clock you don't control."

I'd rather say that than oversell it.
